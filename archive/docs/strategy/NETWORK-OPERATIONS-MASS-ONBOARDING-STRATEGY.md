# NETWORK OPERATIONS & MASS ONBOARDING STRATEGY

## Why this exists
Network density cannot be treated as a purely self-serve software problem.

The company may need a distributed human operations model to launch high-quality networks until organic acquisition becomes dominant.

## Operator role
A Network Operator is trained to:
- acquire organizer permission;
- collect/import approved seed data;
- configure templates/privacy;
- create or assist initial structure;
- onboard 3–10 trusted volunteer admins;
- run claim/verification campaigns;
- demonstrate value;
- seed first-use workflows;
- track activation;
- hand off ownership.

## Operator should not
- collect unnecessary personal information;
- impersonate members;
- approve sensitive facts without authority;
- remain the permanent data-maintenance layer;
- optimize for registrations alone.

## Operator hierarchy
Possible future model:
- Central Network Operations
- Regional Leads
- Community/Vertical Specialists
- Local Operators
- Volunteer Admins / Champions

## Network launch playbook

### Phase 1 — Anchor
Obtain explicit approval from a trusted network authority.

### Phase 2 — Bootstrap
Import only the approved minimum viable roster and structure.

### Phase 3 — Leadership seed
Onboard a small group of respected volunteers/admins.

### Phase 4 — Claim
Ask members to claim and verify profiles.

### Phase 5 — Utility launch
Launch one or two high-value workflows appropriate to the vertical.

Examples:
- Family/community: family completion + matrimonial/community event.
- Alumni: mentoring/referral campaign.
- Franchise: operating-help request.
- Organization: expertise/ownership discovery.
- Business Trust: verified provider request.

### Phase 6 — Contribution
Ask members to fix missing information and relationships.

### Phase 7 — Outcomes
Capture concrete success stories.

### Phase 8 — Expansion
Ask the organizer/admins to invite an adjacent network.

## Operator economics
Track:
- cost per activated network;
- time to first useful outcome;
- time to 50% claimed;
- operator hours per network;
- number of networks handed off successfully;
- 30/90-day retention;
- adjacent networks generated.

The model must become more efficient over time through tooling, templates, automation and community champions.

## Reward architecture
Reward:
- verified activation;
- retention;
- useful contribution;
- successful network-to-network expansion.

Do not reward raw signups.

## Tooling roadmap for operators
Future Operator Console may include:
- onboarding pipeline;
- data import;
- duplicate cleanup;
- verification status;
- invitation campaigns;
- activation checklist;
- network health;
- suggested next actions;
- training content;
- privacy/compliance checklist;
- referral tracking;
- reward tracking.

## Strategic principle
**Operators create network density; the product must ultimately preserve it without them.**
