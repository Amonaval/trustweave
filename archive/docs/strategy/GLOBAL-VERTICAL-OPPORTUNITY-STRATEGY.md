# Generic Network OS — Global Vertical Opportunity Strategy

**Updated:** 2026-08-27  
**Status:** Strategic portfolio hypothesis; not an implementation queue.

## 1. Portfolio correction

Family remains the strongest consumer/product-quality proving ground, but Generic Network OS must not become a Family-only roadmap. The platform should deliberately pursue a portfolio:

1. **Consumer / community wedge** — Family + community where relationship, memory, belonging and trusted identity matter.
2. **B2B monetization wedge** — one or two domains where a network removes recurring operational pain and has a clear buyer.
3. **Institutional distribution wedge** — cases where one anchor organization can activate hundreds or thousands of participants.
4. **Platform moat** — reusable graph/network primitives, governed identity, knowledge/evidence, participation and multi-network interoperability.

Do not build ten verticals at once. Use the architecture to run focused vertical experiments cheaply.

## 2. Evaluation criteria for a new vertical

A new vertical is promising when most are true:
- real-world entities and relationships are naturally graph-shaped;
- trust/provenance materially improves outcomes;
- one person/entity belongs to multiple groups or layers;
- users repeatedly need to find the right person, expertise, path, proof, resource or next action;
- existing workflows are fragmented across chat, spreadsheets, directories and institutional systems;
- a buyer or anchor institution exists;
- the network becomes more useful as participation/data density grows;
- 30–60% of Generic Network OS can be reused without flattening domain semantics;
- optional RAG/LLM can improve knowledge activation without becoming the source of truth.

## 3. Most promising new verticals beyond the current five

### Tier 1 — strongest near-term strategic candidates

#### A. Professional Expertise & Referral Networks
Examples: doctors, lawyers, CAs/accountants, architects, consultants, interior designers, engineers and specialist associations.

**Core pain:** expertise and trusted referrals exist inside professional communities but discovery is fragmented and reputation is hard to verify contextually.

**Differentiated product:**
- verified specialty/expertise graph;
- trusted referral paths;
- geography/jurisdiction/availability-aware discovery;
- case-study / precedent / knowledge evidence where legally and ethically permitted;
- peer questions and governed introductions;
- mentorship and continuing-professional-development communities;
- organization → chapter → member → specialty relationships;
- opt-in cross-network referrals.

**Why promising:** clear professional value, medium/high willingness to pay for associations and practices, natural graph structure, and strong fit with existing identity + relationships + RAG foundations.

**Caution:** healthcare/legal domains have strong privacy, regulatory and professional-conduct constraints. Start with professional networking, expertise and non-sensitive knowledge—not patient/client records.

#### B. Industry / Trade Ecosystem Networks
Examples: manufacturers, distributors, suppliers, service providers, trade associations, industrial clusters, builders and construction ecosystems.

**Core pain:** trusted suppliers, capabilities, certifications, dependencies and prior experience are scattered across spreadsheets, ERP/CRM records, WhatsApp and individual memory.

**Differentiated product:**
- company/entity graph rather than only people graph;
- capability and certification mapping;
- buyer–supplier / dependency / geography relationships;
- trusted introductions and referral provenance;
- request → match → introduction → outcome capture;
- operational knowledge and case-study retrieval;
- risk/concentration visibility;
- cluster/association umbrella networks.

**Why promising:** strong B2B monetization, clear ROI, institutional buyers, and graph + evidence intelligence can become genuinely differentiated.

#### C. Healthcare Provider & Specialist Collaboration Network
Examples: hospital groups, independent hospitals, clinics, labs, specialists, referral partners and Apollo-like multi-hospital ecosystems.

**Core pain:** expertise, referral routes, operational learnings and capability visibility are fragmented across facilities.

**Potential value:**
- hospital/group/facility/department/specialist hierarchy;
- non-patient specialist directory and referral routing;
- capability/equipment/service availability;
- clinical/non-clinical knowledge library with evidence and governance;
- cross-hospital best-practice sharing;
- training/mentorship;
- operational incident learnings;
- location-to-location collaboration;
- franchise/chain-style governance where applicable.

**Long-term RAG fit:** network-aware retrieval across approved protocols, case studies, SOPs, research, internal learnings and expertise profiles.

**Important boundary:** do not make patient diagnosis/treatment or sensitive health records an early wedge. Healthcare raises regulatory, safety and liability requirements substantially.

### Tier 2 — high-potential follow-ons

#### D. Education & Learning Ecosystems
School groups, universities, departments, alumni, mentors, research labs, clubs, parent communities and partner institutions. Strong multi-network identity and lifecycle value from student → alumni → mentor → employer.

#### E. Public-Service / Civic Ecosystem Networks
Government departments, schemes, district/state bodies, officers, service centers, NGOs and implementation partners. Potentially enormous anchor-led rollout, but procurement, policy, privacy and integration complexity are high.

#### F. Nonprofit / Mission / Volunteer Networks
Umbrella NGO → local chapter → volunteer → skill → beneficiary program → partner organization. Strong coordination and institutional-anchor characteristics.

#### G. Real-Estate / Built-Environment Ecosystems
Builders, architects, contractors, consultants, vendors, projects, sites, societies and facility-management networks. Strong entity graph and repeat vendor/expertise discovery.

#### H. Research / Innovation / Expert Networks
Universities, labs, researchers, projects, grants, papers, expertise and industry collaborators. Excellent graph + RAG fit and strong global relevance.

## 4. Global Family is still relevant—but the product framing changes

Family should not assume an Indian joint-family mental model.

A global Family mode should emphasize universally understandable outcomes:
- **Who is this person to me?**
- relationship paths across blended, adopted, step, half and chosen-family structures;
- memories, photos, places and stories;
- family history and ancestry;
- geographically dispersed relatives;
- privacy controls for living people;
- collaborative correction/merge confidence;
- accessibility and mobile-first search;
- heritage, migration and generational continuity;
- optional cultural/religious/custom modules rather than assumptions in the base product.

The platform should support **regional/vertical capability profiles** so cultural features are enabled because they fit the network, not because the product was designed for one country.

## 5. Global product principle

Do not build “India mode” and “rest-of-world mode” as separate products. Build:

**universal network primitives + regional terminology/date/locale/privacy defaults + vertical capability profiles + optional cultural modules.**

## 6. Directional external evidence

- FamilySearch reported its collaborative tree reached about 1.8B searchable people in 2025 and continues investing in mobile, relationship viewing, records and memories across more than 170 countries/principalities. This supports the view that family-history/relationship products are globally relevant, while also proving that a plain family tree is a crowded space.
- Global digital usage remains strongly mobile; mobile devices accounted for roughly half of worldwide web traffic in Q2 2026, while broader internet-use data shows staying in touch with friends/family remains a major global motivation. This reinforces mobile-first product design.

These are directional signals, not proof of willingness-to-pay for Generic Network OS.

## 7. Portfolio recommendation

Do not add another vertical immediately just because it is architecturally easy. First select **two candidate commercial experiments**:

1. **Professional Expertise & Referral Network** — lower regulatory scope first (e.g. CA/accounting, architects/builders, industry experts).
2. **Industry / Trade Ecosystem Network** — manufacturers/suppliers/associations.

Keep Healthcare as a strategically important but higher-risk vertical once security, compliance and evidence-governance maturity justify it.

Family continues in parallel as a consumer/global UX laboratory rather than consuming the entire roadmap.
