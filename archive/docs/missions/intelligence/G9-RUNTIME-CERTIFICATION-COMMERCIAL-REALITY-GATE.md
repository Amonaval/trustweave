## Commercial Reality Gate criteria
A vertical may proceed to serious commercial investment only if it has:
1. a painful recurring job;
2. a buyer with budget/authority;
3. measurable outcome value;
4. a credible reason to use this instead of existing systems/manual work;
5. data onboarding that is tolerable;
6. a pilot that can show value in weeks, not quarters;
7. evidence that the product becomes more useful as outcomes/relationships are captured.

## Primary wedge: Organizational Intelligence
Do **not** position it as generic ONA or another employee directory.

### Paid job
**“Help technical/knowledge-heavy organizations find who knows, who owns, what depends on whom, where knowledge is concentrated, and what is at risk.”**

### Buyer
- CTO / VP Engineering;
- Platform/Architecture leader;
- engineering operations / transformation leader;
- knowledge-management leader in a technical organization.

### First three commercially meaningful workflows
1. **Find the real expert / owner**
   - Who understands Authentication Platform?
   - Who owns this service?
   - Who has solved this class of incident before?

2. **Knowledge concentration / succession risk**
   - Which critical capabilities depend on one or two people?
   - Where do we have no verified backup expertise?

3. **Dependency / coordination path**
   - Which teams/products depend on this capability?
   - Who should be involved before this change?
   - What is the shortest known path to the right owner/expert?

### Why this can win despite existing ONA tools
Established platforms prove that organizational-network analysis and collaboration insight are real categories. Our wedge should avoid competing primarily on meeting/email collaboration analytics. The differentiated product should combine **explicit expertise + ownership + products/projects + typed dependencies + captured lessons + explainable paths** and turn those into actions.

## Franchise challenger
### Paid job
**“Stop every location from solving the same operational problem independently.”**

### Strong workflows
- Which location solved this issue before?
- Who ran that intervention?
- Which playbook/outcome should we reuse?
- Which stores need support or training?
- Where is operational knowledge concentrated?

Large franchise platforms already cover broad operations, training, compliance and AI assistance. Therefore the Network OS should not attempt to replace them. Its wedge would be a **cross-location operating-learning graph** that can complement existing systems.

## Business Trust hold condition
The proposition remains strong — explainable trust and warm sourcing paths — but commercial value depends on trustworthy provenance and enough seeded relationships. Before a paid pilot it needs:
- relationship provenance;
- who verified/recommended whom;
- recency;
- outcome/evidence capture;
- explicit current-user/network start point;
- anti-gaming/governance rules.

## Hard gate before G10
G10 remains blocked until at least one target organization:
- supplies/imports real network data;
- identifies a real recurring workflow;
- completes a pilot with named users;
- demonstrates at least one measurable outcome;
- and gives strong evidence of willingness to pay (paid pilot, signed commercial intent, or equivalent budget-backed commitment).

If buyers like the demo but will not provide data/run a pilot, the product has **not** passed the commercial gate.
